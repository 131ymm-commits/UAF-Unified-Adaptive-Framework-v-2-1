**Layer:** L0–L1

## Problem
Dark matter (~27% of energy density) and dark energy (~68%) have no direct detection despite decades of search. The standard model fine-tuning problem: why is the cosmological constant so small?

## UAF Answer
Dark matter and dark energy are reframed as missing terms in the cosmological free energy rather than guaranteed new substances.

F_cosmo = F_visible + F_dark_matter + F_dark_energy

where:
- F_dark_matter = coherence penalty between visible and hidden levels: λ · D_KL(Π_{L0→L1} Q_{L0} || Q_{L1}). This term contributes to gravitational dynamics without electromagnetic coupling.
- F_dark_energy = topological complexity term γ · T_{L0} — the cost of maintaining the prediction horizon as the universe expands.

The cosmological constant problem is reframed: Λ is not a fine-tuned constant but an emergent equilibrium between the horizon expansion rate and the rate of decoherence of the quantum-classical transition.
