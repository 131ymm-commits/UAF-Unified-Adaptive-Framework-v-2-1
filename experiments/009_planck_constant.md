**Layer:** L0

**Date:** 2025-07-13 / revised 2026-06-03

## Problem
Why does ħ have the value it has? In standard physics it is a free parameter, set by experiment. Fine-tuning: change ħ by a factor of 2 and atoms become unstable. Why this value?

## UAF Answer
ħ is not a free parameter. It is the emergent optimal information exchange rate between L0 (quantum) and L1 (classical) levels.

The precision of quantum prediction is bounded below by the Heisenberg uncertainty:
Δx · Δp ≥ ħ/2

In UAF terms: ħ/2 is the minimum free energy cost of a binary distinction at L0. It is the granularity of the prediction manifold.

The specific value of ħ is the optimum of:
ħ* = arg min_{ħ} [F_L0→L1(ħ) + F_stability(ħ)]

where:
- F_L0→L1: the cost of transmitting prediction errors upward (decreases with larger ħ — coarser grain means fewer distinctions needed)
- F_stability: the cost of maintaining stable L1 structures (atoms, molecules) — increases with larger ħ (too coarse and atoms become unstable)

ħ* is the saddle point of this trade-off. The "unreasonable" precision of the value is explained: any other value collapses the multi-level prediction hierarchy.
