## Problem
Standard economic theory (utility maximization, rational agents, equilibrium) fails to explain: financial crises as surprise events, bounded rationality, power dynamics, inflation as a multi-causal phenomenon.

## UAF Answer — Unified Economic Theory (UET)

Time is the fundamental conserved resource. All economic quantities are derivatives of time.

**Conservation law:**
t_past + t_now + t_future = const (for any agent system)

**Agent time decomposition:**
- t_self: sovereign time (autonomous choice)
- t_market: voluntarily traded time
- t_obligation: biological + contractual constraints
- t_captured: extracted by others (attention economy, coercion)

Balance: t_self + t_market + t_obligation + t_captured = 24h/day

**UAF mapping:**
- Wealth = controlled future time of others: W_J = Σᵢ α_{Ji} · t_{future,i}^controlled
- Money = standardized contract on future time with trust coefficient α
- Inflation = (ΔM_future − ΔT_real) / ΔM_future (money growth outpaces real time creation)
- Power = P_J = Σᵢ α_{Ji} · (Δt_{captured,i} − Δt_{self,i})
- Economic growth = f(Δt_integrated, Δα, Δefficiency)

**Welfare metric — NFTI (Net Free Time Index):**

NFTI = mean(t_self − θ · t_captured) across population

This replaces GDP as the primary welfare indicator. A society with high GDP but high t_captured has low NFTI — captures the attention economy problem.

**UAF free energy formulation:**
The economic agent minimizes expected free energy:
G(π) = Risk + Ambiguity
= 0.5 · ||μ_predicted_outcome − μ_preferred||² + 0.5 · Σ ln(2πe σ²_j)

Utility maximization is a special case: G(π) with zero ambiguity and risk = −utility.

**Testable predictions:**
1. Economies with higher NFTI have more stable long-term growth (lower volatility).
2. Attention-extracting institutions (social media) reduce NFTI without appearing in GDP.
3. Trust coefficient α decline precedes financial crises (free energy spike).
