# Experiment 007 — Big Bang as Phase Transition L−1 → L0
**Layer:** L−1 → L0
**Epistemic status:** Layer 4
**Date:** 2025-07-13 / revised 2026-06-03

## Problem
Why did the universe begin in a low-entropy state? The second law of thermodynamics requires entropy to increase — but this requires a low-entropy starting point that is postulated, not derived.

## UAF Answer
The Big Bang is not an explosion in space. It is a phase transition from atemporal mathematical structure (L−1) to temporal quantum prediction (L0).

At L−1, there is no observation, no prediction horizon Δ, no time. There is only the space of possible generative models P(s).

At L0, a generative model with Δ_{L0} > 0 becomes defined — the first moment where "before" and "after" are distinguishable.

The low initial entropy follows from the transition selecting the minimum-complexity prior consistent with a stable multi-level prediction hierarchy:

P_{L0}(initial) = arg min_{P} K(P) subject to: F_total(P) < F_max for all subsequent levels

The minimum-complexity prior is necessarily a low-entropy state — a highly ordered initial condition is the price of being the simplest possible generative model that can support stable prediction at L1–L8.

This is Penrose's Weyl curvature hypothesis reframed: the initial low Weyl curvature is the statement that the L0 prior has minimal complexity.

## Connection to UAF-3.1
UAF-3.1 formalizes this through the three-component time structure: t_past = 0 at the Big Bang (no crystallized past), t_future = maximum (all possibilities open). The transition increases t_past and decreases t_future as the universe crystallizes.
